--- title: The inaccessible Amazon date: 2025-02-18 teaser: |- foo [thumbnail amazon-inaccessible-map.jpg You can't get here from there.] bar tags: - thoughtstopics/environment - thoughtstopics/mapping - thoughtstopics/transport --- South America is a big place, with a diverse range of natural environments, and an eclectic mix in standards of infrastructure. These days, pretty much everywhere on the continent can be reached from everywhere else by a reasonably good road network. Everywhere, that is, except – unsurprisingly – a vast swathe of the Amazon basin (plus a fair bit of Chilean Patagonia). [thumbnail amazon-inaccessible-map.jpg You can't get here from there.] I was surprised to discover some parts of the Amazon that I thought were unreachable by road, but that actually are well-connected – thanks to recent large-scale highway projects that have, sadly, left mass deforestation in their wake; and, conversely, other parts of the Amazon that I thought were reachable by road, but that actually remain discontiguous with the rest of the continent. Join me as I map out where in the Amazon is and isn't accessible by road (note: I'm beginning my run-down in the south-east corner of the Amazon Basin, and working my way around clockwise from there).

Brasil: Pará

First up, the city of Belém, capital of the sprawling Brazilian state of Pará. Belém is quite easily accessible by road from everywhere further south in Brazil, via a number of roads, most notably the BR-316 (which is part of the Belém-Brasília Highway). There is no road access as such to anywhere north of Belém. However, the contiguous Brazilian highway network could arguably be said to extend slightly further, on the other side of Baía de Marajó, onto Ilha de Marajó. There are (apparently) fairly frequent and fairly fast ferry services from Belém to Soure (the main town on Ilha de Marajó); and then the PA-154 highway (which is apparently now sealed all the way) runs through the south-east corner of the island, from just north of Soure, down to Cachoeira do Arari. However, there are no roads whatsoever on the north / north-west side of the island, and there most definitely is no bridge over the Amazon Delta north of the island. And so, the entire state of Amapá, and its capital city Macapá, which lies on the north bank of the Amazon Delta, is disconnected from Belém and from the rest of Brazil (with its only road connection being to neighbouring French Guiana further north).
